Lab Safety & Safety Rules

Prior to operating the high-power CO2 laser cutting system, mandatory industrial safety protocols and pre-flight checks were strictly executed to prevent fire hazard, toxic fume exposure, and optical injury.

🛡️ Laser Safety

Interlocked acrylic safety lid remained closed during active firing. Class 4 laser protective eyewear verified; beam never inspected directly.

💨 Exhaust System

High-volume exhaust blower engaged before job start to evacuate vaporized acrylic particulates and combustion by-products outside the lab chamber.

❄️ Water Chiller

Industrial CW-5200 water chiller active and verified in the 18°C–22°C operating window before laser tube activation to prevent thermal shock.

⚡ Earthing

Dedicated earth ground verified on the chassis to dissipate high-voltage electrostatic charges and protect digital stepper controllers.

💨 Air Assist

Coaxial compressed air assist engaged continuously at the nozzle tip to quench flare-ups, prevent charring, and keep the focal lens soot-free.

🚨 General Machine Safety

Machine was monitored at all times during cut execution. Emergency stop switch (E-Stop) tested and CO2 fire extinguisher located on standby.

Image-to-DXF Conversion

Raw raster imagery (JPEG/PNG) cannot be directly processed by laser CNC motion controllers because rasters are arrays of pixels rather than coordinate vectors. We used Inkscape / Adobe Illustrator to execute automated vector tracing and manual spline optimization:

  1. Threshold Pre-processing: Grayscale brightness contrast was optimized to eliminate low-frequency compression artifacts.
  2. Trace Bitmap (Potrace Engine): Applied Brightness Cutoff edge detection with smooth corner threshold $(0.85)$ to extract continuous vector paths.
  3. Path Simplification: Reduced redundant Bézier anchor nodes to prevent controller stuttering along curves.
  4. DXF Export: Exported geometry in AutoCAD R14 / 2004 DXF format with $(1.0\text{ mm} = 1.0\text{ mm})$ scale unit preservation.

File Preparation

🧹 Vector Cleaning

Inspected all intersecting path segments and joined open endpoints to ensure 100% boundary continuity.

📐 Scaling & Units

Calibrated true millimeters against machine coordinates to prevent aspect ratio distortion during translation.

⭕ Closed Paths Verification

Executed automatic loop closure checks to ensure the laser cutter would produce complete cutouts without bridge gaps.

✂️ Duplicate Geometry Removal

Eliminated stacked overlapping vectors (double-stroke lines) to prevent accidental double-burning of the kerf.

7

Nesting & Layout in RDWorks

The cleaned DXF file was imported into RDWorks v8. In the workspace:

  • Placement: Anchored design relative to the machine absolute origin $(0,0)$ at top-left.
  • Layer Separation: Internal engravings assigned to Black layer; perimeter cut paths assigned to Red layer.
  • Nesting Efficiency: Nested tightly to material boundaries with a $5.0\text{ mm}$ margin to conserve raw sheet stock.
  • Simulation: Executed virtual preview simulation to verify that internal cutouts process before outer boundaries.

Reflection

During the laser cutting exercise, I gained firsthand experience with the end-to-end workflow of subtractive digital manufacturing. The most significant insight was that physical manufacturing fidelity is governed almost entirely by digital pre-processing: cleaning anchor nodes in vector space and assigning appropriate layer speeds and power ratios prevents material wastage.

In future sessions, I plan to build parametric joint test templates to pre-calculate kerf width offsets for press-fit interlocking assemblies.

4

Why the Object Cannot Be Made Subtractively

Subtractive manufacturing (such as 3-axis CNC milling or laser cutting) relies on linear toolpath access from the workpiece exterior. Our selected 3D geometry features internal re-entrant hollow cavities, organic cantilevered curves, and complex undercuts.

A rotating milling endmill cannot carve hollow enclosed volumes without colliding with the outer walls, nor can a planar 2D laser beam produce 3-dimensional spherical curvature. Additive manufacturing overcomes this fundamentally by depositing material layer by layer from the build plate upward.

5

STL Definition

STL (Standard Tessellation Language) is the industry standard digital format for additive manufacturing. An STL file represents the external 3D surface geometry of a computer-aided design (CAD) model using an interconnected collection of planar triangular facets.

Each triangle is defined by 3 spatial vertices $(x,y,z)$ and a unit normal vector pointing outward. Slicing software imports this watertight polygon mesh and slices it horizontally into 2D toolpath layers (G-code) for the 3D printer.
