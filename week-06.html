Digital Fabrication Sprint

WEEK 6 — LASER CUTTING & 3D PRINTING

A hands-on practical exploration transforming 2D digital artwork and 3D geometric models into physical objects using subtractive (RDWorks) and additive (Bambu Studio) manufacturing technologies.

🔥 Part 1: Laser Cutting 🖨️ Part 2: 3D Printing 🎯 Learning Outcomes
🔥 Subtractive Digital Fabrication

PART 1: LASER CUTTING WITH RDWORKS

We selected an image design, prepared and converted the image into a suitable format, and imported the design into RDWorks. In RDWorks, we arranged and prepared the design for the laser-cutting process before producing the physical model with the laser cutter.

Workflow: Image Selection ➔ Conversion / Preparation ➔ RDWorks Setup ➔ Laser Cutting ➔ Final Model
Step 1 – Selecting the Image

Selecting the Artwork

I started by selecting the image and design that I wanted to reproduce using the laser cutter. The choice focused on clear, high-contrast graphic boundaries that would readily translate into crisp laser paths.

📸 Photo: Original selected image ready for laser path conversion.

📸 Photo: Prepared vector contours and cleaned cutting geometry.

Step 2 – Preparing the Design

Image Conversion & Prep

I prepared and converted the image into a clean vector format. We adjusted the contrast, removed noise, and generated closed outline paths so the machine controller could recognize unambiguous cutting and engraving lines.

Step 3 – RDWorks

Setup inside RDWorks

I imported the prepared design into RDWorks. In RDWorks, the design was positioned relative to the machine origin, and laser parameters including cutting speed (mm/s) and power percentage (%) were configured for accurate execution.

📸 Photo: Screenshot of the design inside RDWorks software.

📸 Photo: Active laser-cutting process on the machine bed.

Step 4 – Laser Cutting

Carrying Out the Cut

After preparing the file, I transferred it to the laser-cutting machine and positioned the material properly on the honeycomb bed. The laser cutter followed the digital paths and accurately reproduced the design.

Step 5 – Final Laser-Cut Model

Completed Laser Part

The finished physical model was extracted from the cutting bed. The edges were clean and well-defined, perfectly replicating the digital vector geometry with tight dimensional accuracy.

📸 Photo: Final laser-cut physical model.

🖨️ Additive Digital Fabrication

PART 2: 3D PRINTING WITH BAMBU STUDIO

We selected a 3D model from Printables.com and downloaded it in STL format. We imported the STL file into Bambu Studio, positioned and prepared the model for printing, sliced the file, and produced the final physical model on the 3D printer.

Workflow: Printable.com ➔ STL File ➔ Bambu Studio ➔ Model Prep & Slicing ➔ 3D Printing ➔ Final Model
Step 1 – Selecting the 3D Model

Discovering on Printable.com

I started by browsing and selecting a functional 3D design from Printables.com that was well-optimized for additive FDM printing.

📸 Photo: Selected 3D model on Printables.com.

📸 Photo: Downloaded 3D geometry in STL format.

Step 2 – STL File

Downloading the STL File

I downloaded the model in **STL format**, which represents the 3D surface geometry through interconnected triangular meshes, making it ready for digital slicing.

Step 3 – Bambu Studio

Importing into Bambu Studio

I imported the STL file into **Bambu Studio**. Bambu Studio converted the imported model into a printable setup and placed it squarely onto the virtual build plate.

📸 Photo: Model inside the Bambu Studio workspace.

📸 Photo: Bambu Studio slicing and preparation stage.

Step 4 – Preparing & Slicing

Positioning, Infill & Slicing

I checked the model orientation and prepared it for printing. The model was sliced into printable horizontal layers so that the 3D printer could build the object layer by layer.

Step 5 – 3D Printing

3D Printing Execution

Finally, the prepared model was sent to the 3D printer. The printer extruded molten filament through the heated nozzle onto the build plate, progressively materializing the object.

📸 Photo: 3D printer actively building the physical model.

📸 Photo: Final 3D printed physical model.

Step 6 – Final 3D Model

Completed 3D Print

Through this activity, I understood the complete basic workflow of 3D printing — from an STL file → preparing the model in Bambu Studio → slicing → physical 3D print.

🎯 Practical Synthesis

WEEK 6 LEARNING OUTCOME

This week's activities helped me understand the practical workflow of digital design to physical manufacturing. I learned how to prepare images for laser cutting using RDWorks and how to work with STL files and Bambu Studio for 3D printing.

🔥 Subtractive (Laser Cutting)

Digital Design → RDWorks → Laser Cutter → Physical Design

Learned the importance of vector path preparation, contrast adjustments, and setting machine speed and power layers.

🖨️ Additive (3D Printing)

STL File → Bambu Studio → Slicing → 3D Printer → Physical Model

Understood 3D triangular meshes, bed orientation, infill configurations, and slicing layer by layer into G-code.
